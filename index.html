Tower defense is a subgenre of strategy video games in which the player defends a base or territory against waves of enemies by placing defensive structures, called towers, along or near the enemies' route. Defeating enemies earns in-game currency, which the player spends on more towers and upgrades, while enemies that get through usually cost the player lives.

This wiki explains the genre and documents the browser game made for this project. You can try the game on the Play page, or read about its towers, enemies and upgrades and strategy.

Gameplay

In a typical tower defense game, enemies travel in waves towards the player's base. The player does not control the enemies or, usually, the towers once they are built. Instead the decisions are where to place towers, which kind to build and when to spend or save currency.[1]

Common elements include:

  • Waves – groups of enemies, usually with a set number and type per wave, that get harder over time.
  • Towers – defences with different ranges, damage and fire rates. Some slow enemies or damage an area.
  • Currency – earned by defeating enemies and spent on building and upgrading towers.
  • Lives – each enemy that reaches the end of the route costs the player lives. At zero, the game is lost.
  • Mazing – in games where towers block the way, the tactic of building a long, winding path of towers so that enemies must travel further.[1]

History

Precursors

Earlier arcade and home games such as Space Invaders (1978) and Missile Command (1980) already centred on defending a position against waves of attackers, and contained ideas the genre later built on. Rampart (1990) is generally considered to have established the prototypical tower defense game, with player-placed defences and separate build, defend and repair phases.[1]

Custom maps

In the early 2000s the genre spread through scenarios made by players for real-time strategy games. Early examples for StarCraft: Brood War were Turret Defense (May 2000) and Sunken Defense (November 2001). Warcraft III (2002) added role-playing elements to such scenarios, and Element TD and Gem Tower Defense (2006) were built with its World Editor.[1]

The browser boom

From 2007, independent developers using Adobe Flash and the arrival of smartphone app stores helped the genre grow quickly. Desktop Tower Defense (March 2007) and Bloons Tower Defense (2007) were early browser hits, and Plants vs. Zombies (May 2009) became a highly popular series on mobile devices.[1]

Modern era

Kingdom Rush (2011) sold more than seventeen million copies across the App Store and Play Store. Other games mixed the genre with action, such as Dungeon Defenders (2010), which used a third-person view, and Anomaly: Warzone Earth (2011), which introduced “reverse tower defense” in which the player attacks protected bases.[1]

This project's example game

The game on this site is a tower defense game written in JavaScript with the Phaser 3 library, so it runs directly in a web browser. The player starts with 100 gold and 15 lives and defends against endless, increasingly tough waves. Enemies follow a fixed path, so there is no mazing; strategy comes from tower choice, placement and timing instead.

  • Towers – 5 types, including area damage, slowing and marching troops.
  • Enemies – 6 types, including a brood that bursts into three minions and a boss every fifth wave.
  • Upgrades and strategy – choose one of three upgrades every 4 waves.
